\section{An artificial muon sample}

The 1000 protons of \cref{ch:production} send only a handful of muons
through the plane behind the hadron absorber, too few to see what the
detectors make of them. In SHiP's own studies, stage~2 replays the plane
particles of a production of many millions of protons through the muon shield
and the detector. That is the same job as the one below with a different
source, and you can try it on your own stage-1 file at the end of this chapter
(\cref{ex:ownreplay}); here we start from an \emph{artificial} sample instead.

\file{code/workflows/muon\_gun.jsonnet} fires 100 muons, one per event, from
the beam axis at the back of the muon shield, $z=27$\,m, at most 20\,mrad
from the beam direction, towards the decay volume and the detectors behind
it. Their momenta are flat between 10 and 100\,GeV and they all have the same
charge, so the sample shows what the detectors see of a muon, not how many
muons SHiP will see. With respect to stage~1, two more things change:
\begin{itemize}
  \item there is no scoring plane;
  \item the detectors are sensitive, and the field maps of the muon shield and
        the spectrometer magnet are switched on (\cref{sec:fieldservice}).
\end{itemize}

\codefile[linerange={29-134},basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize]{jsonnet}{code/workflows/muon_gun.jsonnet}

\texttt{particle\_gun\_source} writes the muon into \texttt{mc\_particles},
as \texttt{fixed\_target\_source} did for the products of a proton collision;
Geant4 gives it \field{track\_id}~1 and \field{parent\_id}~0, which is how the
analysis of \cref{ch:analysis} finds it.

\section{Sensitive volumes}
\label{sec:sensitive}

In the default \emph{scoring} mode, Geant4 creates a \texttt{SimHit} for every
step that deposits energy inside a \emph{sensitive} volume. \pkg{aegir} makes
a logical volume sensitive when its name \emph{contains} one of the patterns
in \texttt{sensitive\_volumes}, and gives its hits the \field{detector\_id} of
that entry. An entry is either a bare pattern that \pkg{aegir} knows
(\texttt{trackers}, \texttt{calorimeter}, \ldots), or
\texttt{\{pattern: \ldots, detector: \ldots\}}, which names the detector
itself; this workflow uses the second form throughout. Three consequences:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The detector of a hit is what the entry says, whatever the order of
        the list. \pkg{Shannon} later chooses the digitiser from
        \field{detector\_id} (\cref{ch:datamodel}).
  \item A pattern selects everything whose name contains it, including
        passive volumes with similar names. Here the patterns name the active
        volumes (tiles, straw gas, scintillator bars and fibres, timing bars),
        except for the SBT, whose cells and the aluminium walls around them
        share their prefix (\cref{ex:sensitive}).
  \item Volumes that match no pattern produce no hits at all. If a detector is
        missing from the list, it is silently invisible.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{pitfall}
A pattern that \pkg{aegir} does not know, or an unknown detector name, stops
the job while the configuration is read, before Geant4 starts: that is the
error to expect if you mistype a detector. A mistyped \emph{pattern} in the
\texttt{\{pattern, detector\}} form is not caught: it simply matches nothing.
\end{pitfall}

\begin{note}
\pkg{aegir}'s own workflows for the SHiP geometry ship with
\texttt{sensitive\_volumes: ['ScoringPlane']}, which matches nothing in the
real detector: they are set up for flux studies at scoring planes. Stage~1 of
this tutorial has an empty list for the same reason.
\end{note}

\section{Running it}

\begin{exercise}{Simulate the muons}
\label{ex:muongun}
In the \pkg{aegir} environment, in \file{work/}, which must contain
\file{ship\_geometry.db} and the two field maps:
\begin{shell}
cd "$SHIP_TUTORIAL/work"
time phlex -j 4 -c <(jsonnet $CODE/workflows/muon_gun.jsonnet)
\end{shell}
Watch the log: the field maps are loaded and attached to their volumes, and
Geant4 reports its progress every ten events.
\end{exercise}

\begin{checkpoint}
Two new files, \file{muon\_gun.root} and \file{muon\_gun\_validation.root},
and no error. \file{muon\_gun.root} has 100 entries, one per muon, with the
six fields of \cref{sec:filelayout}.
\end{checkpoint}

\begin{exercise}{Read the validation histograms}
Open \file{muon\_gun\_validation.root} with a ROOT browser, as in
\cref{ch:production}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{h\_hit\_detector}: which detector IDs occur, and roughly in
        which proportions? Is any of the five missing?
  \item \texttt{h\_hit\_momentum}: compare with the momentum range of the
        gun. Which particles make the hits below 10\,GeV?
  \item \texttt{h\_mc\_multiplicity}: what does it show here, and why?
\end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All five occur. The straws (2) have the most hits: a muon crossing a
        station leaves hits in many straw layers, and almost every muon at
        20\,mrad crosses all four stations. The SBT (1) has many hits too, although
        few muons reach it: a muon crossing its liquid scintillator travels
        metres in it and leaves a hit at every step. In the dry run, 100 muons
        gave about 4100 straw and 3400 SBT hits. The UBT (0), the timing
        detector (4) and the calorimeter (3) see nearly every muon, with
        fewer hits each.
  \item The muons between 10 and 100\,GeV, less what they lost on the way;
        the hits below 10\,GeV are from secondaries, mostly $\delta$-rays.
  \item One entry at 1: the gun makes one particle per event.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\section{Reproducibility and threads}

\begin{exercise}{Same seed, same events}
Rename your output, run the same command again, and compare selected per-event
summaries with \file{sim\_summary.py}. Then run once more with \cmd{-j 1} and
once with \cmd{--tla-code seed=1}.
\begin{shell}
mv muon_gun.root muon_gun_run1.root
# ... the phlex command again ...
python $CODE/analysis/sim_summary.py muon_gun_run1.root muon_gun.root
\end{shell}
Which summaries agree exactly, and which differ? Equal summaries are a
smoke check, not proof that every field in the two files is identical.
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
For a fixed software build and identical inputs, the expected result is
matching event content with the same seed, also with \cmd{-j 1}: the gun and
Geant4 are reseeded per event from (seed, event index), so neither the number
of threads nor the order in which events finish changes any event. The
entries can still be in a different order in the file, which is why
\file{sim\_summary.py} compares per-event quantities keyed by
\field{event\_number}. The run with \cmd{seed=1} has other muons, event by
event, from the same distributions.
\end{solution}

\section{Going further}

\begin{exercise}[colback=white]{Switch the field off}
\label{ex:nofield}
Simulate the same muons without any field:
\begin{shell}
phlex -j 4 -c <(jsonnet --tla-code field=false \
    --tla-str output=muon_gun_nofield $CODE/workflows/muon_gun.jsonnet)
\end{shell}
Keep \file{muon\_gun\_nofield.root}: you will compare it with the field-on
sample in the event display (\cref{ch:display}) and in the analysis
(\cref{ch:analysis}).
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[colback=white]{Optional: the other charge}
\label{ex:mup}
Simulate $\mu^+$ with the same seed:
\begin{shell}
phlex -j 4 -c <(jsonnet --tla-code pdg=-13 \
    --tla-str output=muon_gun_mup $CODE/workflows/muon_gun.jsonnet)
\end{shell}
Each event starts with the same momentum and direction as in
\file{muon\_gun.root}, only the charge differs. Where should the two samples
differ, and where not?
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
The gun draws from its own random stream, which does not depend on the
particle type, so the muons start identically. Upstream of the spectrometer
magnet the two samples agree up to Geant4's randomness; in the magnet the
field bends the two charges to opposite sides, so the hits in straw
stations 3 and 4 move in opposite directions in $x$ (the field is vertical).
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[colback=white]{Optional: replay your own stage 1}
\label{ex:ownreplay}
SHiP's real stage~2 replays the plane particles of a production through the
shield and the detector: \file{code/workflows/ft\_replay.jsonnet} does that
with the same detectors and field as \file{muon\_gun.jsonnet}, but with
\texttt{file\_source} reading \texttt{plane\_particles}. Replay your
\file{ft\_production.root}:
\begin{shell}
COUNT="$SHIP_TUTORIAL/aegir/scripts/count_entries.py"
N=$(python $COUNT ft_production.root)   # entries of the stage-1 file
phlex -j 4 -c <(jsonnet --tla-str infile=ft_production.root \
    --tla-code events=$N $CODE/workflows/ft_replay.jsonnet)
python $CODE/analysis/muon_background.py ft_replay.root --pot $POT
\end{shell}
What reaches the detectors from your 1000 protons? How many protons would
you need for the 100 muons of the artificial sample?
\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
Few or no muons: the table of \cref{ex:planesummary} gives the muons per
proton at the plane, and only a fraction of them gets past the shield. In
the dry run, the 3 muons at the plane were all swept away: none reached the
UBT.
Dividing 100 by that rate gives the protons needed at the plane alone; for 100
muons \emph{in the detectors}, divide by the fraction that the shield lets
through as well. That is why SHiP produces stage~1 once, in large batches,
and replays it many times.
\end{solution}

\begin{pitfall}
The driver creates exactly \texttt{events} events whether or not the input has
that many entries. Past the end of the file, \texttt{file\_source} publishes
\emph{empty} events. Always ask for at most as many events as the input holds,
which is what \file{count\_entries.py} tells you.
\end{pitfall}
