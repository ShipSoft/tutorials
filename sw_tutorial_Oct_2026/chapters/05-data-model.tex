\section{Plain structs, written as RNTuples}

\repo{data-model} defines the classes every package exchanges. They are
deliberately simple: plain C++ structs with default values, no methods to
speak of, no pointers between objects, and ROOT dictionaries so that they can
be written to and read from ROOT files. Relations are expressed by
\emph{indices} (a mother index into the same particle vector, a Geant4 track
ID), never by pointers, which keeps the files readable from any language.

The files are written as \href{https://root.cern/doc/latest/classROOT_1_1RNTuple.html}{RNTuples},
ROOT's newer columnar I/O format, designed for efficient reading and writing, with
with a stable on-disk format since ROOT~6.34. An RNTuple has named
\emph{fields}; a field can be a number, a struct or a \texttt{std::vector} of
structs, and every member of a struct is itself a (sub)field.

Dimensional floating-point quantities use \texttt{double}; IDs and statuses
are integers. The storage units are:
\textbf{mm, GeV, ns}. The headers also provide typed views
(\file{SHiP/QuantityView.hpp}) that attach physical units at compile time;
\pkg{aegir} uses them internally, but you do not need them to read a file.

\subsection{Generator particles}
\label{sec:mcstatus}

\texttt{MCParticle::status} follows the HepMC convention. Generator adapters
map their native status codes to it; Pythia\,8 internal status codes are not
copied unchanged. What matters here:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl}
\toprule
\field{status} & meaning \\
\midrule
1 & final state: not decayed by the generator. \textbf{Only these are given to Geant4.} \\
2 & decayed by the generator \\
4 & incoming beam particle \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Mother links point into the same vector: \field{mother\_id} is the index of
the first mother, or $-1$; \field{mothers} lists all of them. The scoring
plane of \cref{ch:production} writes its particles as \texttt{MCParticle}s
too, so that the next stage can read them like generator particles: the
tracks as they reached the plane with status~1, their ancestors with other
statuses.

\begin{pitfall}
\texttt{MCParticle::energy} is the \emph{total} energy;
\texttt{SimParticle::energy} is the \emph{kinetic} energy. Compute invariant
masses from \texttt{MCParticle}s, or recompute $E$ from $|\vec p|$ and the
mass.
\end{pitfall}

\subsection{Simulated particles and hits}

Every particle Geant4 tracks and records becomes a \texttt{SimParticle}. Its
\field{track\_id} is the Geant4 track ID, unique \emph{within one event}; the
particles that came from the source have \field{parent\_id}~$=0$, and every
secondary points to the track that created it. \field{creator\_process} is the
Geant4 process sub-type that created it.

In the default \emph{scoring} mode, a Geant4 step inside a sensitive volume
becomes a \texttt{SimHit} if its deposited energy is positive. In
\emph{crossing} mode a hit records the first step of a track in the volume,
with no energy deposit. A scoring plane records every track that reaches it.
\field{track\_id} says which particle made a hit, and \field{detector\_id}
which detector it is in, with the numbering fixed by the data model:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cl}
\toprule
\field{detector\_id} & detector \\
\midrule
0 & upstream background tagger (UBT) \\
1 & surround background tagger (SBT) \\
2 & straw tubes \\
3 & calorimeter \\
4 & timing detector \\
100 & a scoring plane of \pkg{aegir} (its id in \field{geometry\_node\_id}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{pitfall}
\pkg{Shannon} digitises IDs 0--4 and raises ``No digitiser registered for
detector ID'' for any other. Scoring-plane hits (100) are therefore for
analysis only: the files you digitise in \cref{ch:digitisation} come from a
stage without planes.
\end{pitfall}

\section{What the files look like}
\label{sec:filelayout}

Two kinds of file appear in this tutorial. All of them hold their data in
RNTuples inside an ordinary ROOT file.

\paragraph{Simulation output} (\file{ft\_production.root} and
\file{muon\_gun.root}, from \pkg{aegir}). One RNTuple, \texttt{events}, one
entry per written event (types in namespace \texttt{SHiP}, vectors are
\texttt{std::vector}):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l l L}
\toprule
field & type & content \\
\midrule
\texttt{event\_number}    & \texttt{uint64\_t}                   & the event's number in this job \\
\texttt{event\_header}    & \texttt{EventHeader}                & weight, origin \\
\texttt{mc\_particles}    & \texttt{vector<MCParticle>}    & what the source gave Geant4 \\
\texttt{sim\_hits}        & \texttt{vector<SimHit>}        & detector and plane hits \\
\texttt{sim\_particles}   & \texttt{vector<SimParticle>}   & tracked particles \\
\texttt{plane\_particles} & \texttt{vector<MCParticle>}    & for a replay; empty without a recording plane \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Digitisation output} (\file{ft\_digi.root}, from \pkg{Shannon}).
Seven RNTuples, one per collection, each with a single field \texttt{hit} and
\emph{one entry per object} (a hit, or a particle for \texttt{sim\_particles}):
\texttt{ubt\_hits}, \texttt{sbt\_hits}, \texttt{straw\_tubes\_hits},
\texttt{calorimeter\_hits}, \texttt{timing\_detector\_hits}, and copies of
the input as \texttt{sim\_hits} and \texttt{sim\_particles}.
\Cref{ch:digitisation} discusses what this layout means for an analysis.

\begin{note}
ROOT reads these files through the data-model dictionaries, which the pixi
environment makes available automatically; that is what lets you write
\texttt{p.pdg\_code} on a particle in Python. Outside the environment,
\href{https://github.com/scikit-hep/uproot5}{uproot} can read the same
members column by column without any SHiP library. You will open your first
file in \cref{ex:openfile}, as soon as you have made one.
\end{note}

\section{Keeping old files readable}

Backward readability is a project goal checked with frozen reference files
and a committed schema snapshot, but it has known exceptions. Simulation
files written with data model 0.5 can still be read by \pkg{aegir} and
\pkg{Shannon} built against 0.6, which open them with the right conversion
rules. Opening such a file from PyROOT by path alone can abort with a ROOT
error, and the detector-wrapper rename \field{recHit} to \field{rec\_hit} has
no conversion rule: an old digitised file cannot be read with 0.6. Everything
in this tutorial is produced from scratch with 0.6, so none of this applies
here; see \file{data-model/tests/data/README.md} if you need old files.
